ONE FIELD. ONE RUN.
THE DOOR SAYS SO.
The front screen is not a menu — it is the bottom of today's field. The camera idles below rung zero, today's first planets visible and breathing above, and the craft waits on the pad. Pressing anywhere begins the run: the front door and the game share one verb. Everything else — modes, standings, yesterday — is a hold away, on glass cards over this same living field. You never leave the world; you only park cards on it.
WHY THE LEADERBOARD EARNS ONE MORE DAY, NOT ONE MORE TRY
Ranks are altitude. The board is drawn as the field itself — names planted at the height they reached, scores at their death or top-out points. Your near-miss is a visible 40 metres, not an abstract 3 places.
The ghost is the rematch. Any name's recipe replays as a CORE ghost tomorrow-you can race. "One more day" has a face.
Percentile over position. TOP 4% reads as pride at rank 139 of 3 481; position alone reads as defeat. Both shown, percentile leads.
Friends are a rung, not a tab. The friend band lives inside the same field view at your altitude — the people you actually race are always in frame.
THE FRONT DOOR AND THE STANDINGS — SAME FIELD, ONE CARD APART
THE FRONT DOOR
The bottom of today's field, live: real first planets (scouting the opening is part of the daily ritual), boundary walls already present, craft on the pad at rung zero. One blinking verb; the social line whispers the day's stakes. No logo screen, no menu tree — the title floats over the sky like a constellation label.
THE STANDINGS — RANKS ARE ALTITUDE
The board is the field: names planted where they died or topped out, the friend band highlighted at your height, and the gap to the next friend drawn in ION with its true size — +1 363 · ONE SWING. That line is the entire retention design: the near-miss is measured in the game's own units, and the rematch (their ghost) is one tap away.
THE RITUAL — WHAT A DAY LOOKS LIKE
Open → the door shows today's opening bodies (scouting is free and part of the fun) → PRESS TO FLY → one run → debrief or topped-out card → standings with the gap drawn → share or race a ghost → the reseed countdown starts the appetite for tomorrow. Every step is either the field or one glass card over it; there is no screen in the game that isn't the world.
GHOST RACING — THE LEADERBOARD'S VERB
Every entry is a recipe (Direction 10), so every name is playable: tap one and tomorrow's field offers their ghost — a CORE-white craft flying their exact line, your instruments live against theirs. The board stops being a ranking you read and becomes a queue of opponents you choose. Verification comes free: a score without a valid recipe simply can't exist on the board.
NAMES — THE VOICE EXTENDS
Pilot handles are single words, 3–8 characters, rendered in tracked caps: VELA, KESTREL, MARA. No avatars, no flags, no clan tags — on this board a player is a name at an altitude, same as a planet. Dead runs show hollow DUSK rings (the spent vocabulary again); topped-out runs sit above the gold line. The field names itself daily from a curated list — THE LONG CLIMB, THIN AIR, THE NARROWS — one more thing two players can say to each other about the same day.
