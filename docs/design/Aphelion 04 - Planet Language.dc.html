APHELION  ·  DIRECTION 04  ·  THE PLANET LANGUAGE
A PLANET IS A LAMP,
NOT A ROCK.
The prototype's planets are shaded spheres — simulation vocabulary, and shading implies a light source somewhere in depth, which this world doesn't have. The replacement: flat vector anatomy that emits its own identity. No gradients, no terminator, no 3D. And one new idea doing most of the work: the tide — a bright limb segment that faces the craft, always. Gravity made visible. The planet notices you before you press, works under you while you orbit, and goes dark when you've taken what it had.
EASY PATHS, REFUSED
Soft gradient shading → flat disc + concentric internal structure. Side-on has no light source to shade from.
Texture / craters / noise → anatomy. Internal rings and a core, like a cross-section — a diagram that glows, matching the compass it sits inside.
Full-colour fill → identity lives on the rim and core only. A filled disc would compete with the craft; the body stays near-VOID so the player stays brightest.
Static props → four live states. A planet is always telling you its relationship to you: ahead, in reach, held, spent.
ANATOMY — A CROSS-SECTION THAT EMITS
Rim — the identity hue, always on. The planet is a lamp seen edge-on; its colour is the same colour its compass ring wears, so target and window never need a legend.
Tide — a brighter limb segment that faces the craft at all times, on every planet in range. It is the gravity vector drawn on the thing that owns it. Press, and the tide flares into the grab filament (Direction 01, frame 1). While you orbit, it races around the rim under you. And it scales with mass: a heavy body reaches for you with a longer, brighter, tighter-tracking tide — gravity strength read at a glance, no gauge.
Strata + core — concentric internal rings at low alpha and a soft core dot. Structure without texture: reads as a living diagram at speed, echoes the compass rings around it. Body types replace the core with their glyph — the same glyph the compass ring shows.
Scale rule: rim 2.5px and tide 4px are constant in design px regardless of planet radius — small bodies read as bright rings, giants as thin luminous horizons. Mass is size; nothing else changes.
LIVE — THE TIDE TRACKS THE CRAFT
The bright limb stays under the craft with a slight fluid lag, and an inner stratum ripples after it. The planet is working. Tweak tideLag to tune how liquid it feels.
FOUR STATES — THE PLANET'S RELATIONSHIP TO YOU, ALWAYS LEGIBLE
AHEAD · E0–E1
Distant: rim at 40%, structure faint, no tide. Present and identifiable, but quiet — the field ahead is a constellation of dim coloured rings, not a row of grey balls.
IN REACH · E1+TIDE
The craft enters grab range: rim wakes to full E1 and the tide appears, facing the craft. The planet that would catch your press is the one already reaching for you — the press is taught without a word.
HELD · E2 — THE ANCHOR
Full brightness, core lit, tide racing under the orbit. The compass draws itself around this glow. Correction to Direction 01: the held planet is alive, not grey — it goes to DUSK only after release.
SPENT · DUSK
Released: the lamp goes out — rim to DUSK, core hollow, tide gone. Its light didn't vanish; it moved into the chain, and the craft's bloom is wider for it. A spent field behind you is the run's scoreboard, drawn in the world.
BODY TYPES — THE COMPASS GLYPH, AT PLANETARY SCALE
STANDARD
one core
BINARY
twin cores
PULSAR
core beats · beam ticks
RINGED
flat band — no tilt, no depth
BLACK HOLE
true black body · no core
One anatomy, one variable each — the region discipline applied to bodies. The core is the type slot: standard has one, binary two, pulsar's beats, black hole's is absent (the only body filled with true #000 outside the anomaly — it reads as a hole in the world). Black holes wear AURORA violet — the one exception to the identity band: violet means strange. It belongs to the anomaly and to the bodies that shouldn't exist, so the player's spine learns one association: purple = the rules are different here. The compass ring wears the matching miniature, so a body you've met once is recognizable in the instrument before it's on screen.
NAMING — RETIRING THE TELEMETRY VOICE
P11 · P19 · P12
Status quo. Reads as debug output — hard-sim register, and the source of the label-collision garbage.
12
Recommended: hue + glyph carry identity in-run; the number is an altitude address, shown only when needed. Numbers are assigned bottom-to-top in the seeded field, so every player's "23" is the same body that day. In-run chips show a number in exactly one case: two live targets too close in hue. Post-run, numbers own the stage — results, routes, shared recipes.
KILN · HARROW
Authored names — reserved. Too much voice for a procedural daily field, exactly right for adventure regions' signature bodies. A region's black hole deserves a name; the 14th jade planet of Tuesday does not.
Doctrine: in the run, a planet's name is its colour; in the retelling, it's its altitude. Addresses stay small, utility-face, chip-bound, and appear mid-play only to break a hue tie. Nothing floats loose near a planet ever again.
