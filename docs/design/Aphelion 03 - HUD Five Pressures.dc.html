APHELION  ·  DIRECTION 03  ·  THE IN-RUN HUD — ONE LAYOUT, FIVE PRESSURES
Velocity is the headline.
Everything else orbits it.
Everything is drawn in world space, in design coordinates — identical composition on every device, nothing on a letterbox bar. The layout never changes between states; only the pressure does. The bottom third belongs to the thumb: nothing readable lives there, ever.
WHAT MOVED WHERE — AND WHY
Velocity — top-left, display face, largest type in the game. It governs every capture, release, and boundary call. Promoted from the results sheet to the masthead.
Score → BANK — demoted to a utility-face chip, top-right. It governs no decision. It dims while coasting: nothing is confiscated, it is simply not earning.
Fuel — off the bottom-left corner, onto the craft: a halo arc that doubles as a light source. The cause and the gauge finally share a pixel. It turns ION pink only when it can cost you the run.
Chain — live at last: ×N beside velocity, and physically visible as the craft's bloom radius.
Awards — world-space, at the compass dot that earned them (Direction 02). The top band now holds exactly two readables.
FIVE PRESSURES — SAME LAYOUT EVERY FRAME · 1170×2532 DESIGN SPACE, SHOWN AT 330×716
1 · FREE FLIGHT
Coasting. BANK dims to 55% — earning nothing, losing nothing, a fact not a scold. Planets show a lit limb facing the craft: the world notices you (Direction 04 builds on this). Off-screen bodies are edge dots in identity hue — no labels, no collisions. Fuel is the halo arc on the craft.
2 · CAPTURED · ORBITING
The compass at rest: windows E1, hand thin, crossing dots quiet. The whole instrument sits in the upper two-thirds — during a hold the thumb (shown) covers only trail the craft has already left. Labels ride their arc tips.
3 · PEAK · NEAR RELEASE
The hand closes on the dot; window at E2, hand thick, ghost bright. Velocity heats with the moment — it is about to be cashed. BANK states the armed value: a fact, not an instruction to release.
4 · NEAR THE BOUNDARY
The edge is a risk gradient, not a wall: the ION bloom scales with closing speed, and scoring motes (×3 band) glimmer in the outer band — the reward is signposted, never prompted. Fuel arc has gone ION: low enough to matter. Velocity subline states the fact.
5 · INSIDE AN ANOMALY
The reference standard, untouched: aurora curtains over true black, planets reading through the tint. Nothing about the HUD changes — the world changed, not the instruments. Chip backgrounds go true black so labels hold against the curtains.
WARNINGS — ONE HUE, THREE ENERGIES, SAME GRAMMAR AS THE COMPASS
The prototype's yellow-low / red-empty / red-skull ladder becomes ION at three energies — E1 pulse (0.8Hz), E2 strobe (2Hz), E3 strobe + word. Yellow would add a fourth meaning to hue; severity is ordinal, so it rides the energy channel like everything else. If it's pink, it can cost you the bank — one rule, no exceptions.
The skull goes. A skull judges; SOS states a fact — a distress signal is diegetic, arcade, and already the game's voice ("LEFT THE FIELD AT THE RIGHT WALL · ON FIRE").
The deadline track is the compass inverted. Green windows on orbits say release here; the ION window on your own projected line says a press here still saves you, its dot the last possible moment. Same window-and-dot grammar the player already reads — which means it also quietly teaches that the press is steering, before the first orbit ever forms.
LOW · E1 PULSE
The halo arc is already ION; it breathes at 0.8Hz with a soft outer ring. The number is the label — no icon, no banner.
EMPTY · E2 STROBE
The arc hollows to DUSK structure — spent, like a taken planet — while an ION ring strobes at 2Hz. The craft itself never dims: the player is still the brightest thing alive.
DEADLINE TRACK · E3
On an out-of-bounds trajectory: the ION window rides the ship's own projected line, dot = last possible recovery press. Press inside it and you survive a flaming burn through the edge; miss it and SOS strobes at the craft until the burn-up. Past the dot the line goes dashed — the future thins out. Fuel couples by luminance, never geometry: the window is drawn at its true physical size, but only the portion the tank can afford stays lit — half a tank lights the early half; an empty tank shows the whole window in DUSK. A moment exists, and you cannot buy it.
