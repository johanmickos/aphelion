Aphelion  ·  Design Directions  ·  01 – 12

Twelve directions,
one instrument.

Every page below is a design canvas — a single wide artboard arguing one decision about how Aphelion looks, reads, and pays out. They share a palette and a grammar: brightness is commitment, colour is identity, pink is risk, green is sanctuary. Read them next to VISION.md, which says what the game is for; these say what it looks like when it is.

The Language

01 · 04 · 05 · 08
01
Tokens + Compass
Brightness is commitment. Colour is identity. Pink is risk.
The token system and the signature element — a white-hot craft inside a set of coloured windows. Nothing glows as decoration; everything luminous is stored energy or a fact about the future.
Foundation · palette
04
Planet Language
A planet is a lamp, not a rock.
Flat vector anatomy that emits its own identity — no gradients, no terminator, no implied depth. Plus the tide: a bright limb segment that always faces the craft. Gravity made visible.
Foundation · bodies
05
Living Field
You climb through a medium, not past a backdrop.
Altitude rungs every 25m, perpendicular to travel — a ladder, not a floor. They bow toward mass, part around the craft, and sweep past as you climb. The Geometry Wars tribute, minus the horizon.
Foundation · world
08
Scoring Constitution
The field pays for progress. Skill sets the price. The bank remembers.
Five axioms every number answers to. Progress is the only base currency, skill only multiplies, coasting is unpaid but never punished — and no rule may score what no instrument announced.
Foundation · economy

The Run

02 · 03 · 06 · 07 · 12
02
Release Storyboard
The pause is the punch. The word lands on the dot.
The 400ms payoff, frame by frame: a 70ms hitstop where only the player's energy stays lit, every motion along the exit tangent, and the award landing at the dot that earned it.
In-run · the moment
03
HUD — Five Pressures
Velocity is the headline. Everything else orbits it.
One layout that never changes between states; only the pressure does. Velocity to the masthead, score demoted to a chip, fuel moved onto the craft as a halo — and the bottom third kept clear.
In-run · layout
06
Awards + Callouts
Points for the make. Words for the mastery.
A vocabulary of exactly three — TRUE, SHARP, PERFECT — on the rarity ladder players already know. The baseline tier speaks in points alone, and streaks escalate by counting, not by synonym.
In-run · praise
07
Boundary
The edge is a place, not a wall.
Three bands of increasing heat and reward. Intensity tracks closing speed rather than proximity, so the edge answers "how fast are you approaching" — a risk gradient you aim at, not a vignette you flee.
In-run · risk
12
The Finish Line
The carpet takes you home.
The boundary's mirror: same grammar, opposite verdict. Cross into the safety carpet and the run is mathematically over — gravity lets go and the lift throws you past the top of the screen. Green becomes sanctuary.
In-run · the win

The Aftermath

09 · 10 · 11
09
The Debrief
A card over the crash site.
A translucent modal floating over the still-living field — embers settling, boundary still breathing, your bank ring planted at the death altitude. 1.6 seconds, then the insert-coin blink. Arcade cabinets cut; they do not fade.
After · death
10
Results Sheet
The route is the trophy.
A score is a claim; a route is a proof. The run drawn as a light-line through the whole field, composed inside a 4:5 safe frame — plus a recipe line, so a shared run is a replayable fact rather than a picture of one.
After · share
11
Daily Front + Leaderboard
One field. One run. The door says so.
The front screen is not a menu — it is the bottom of today's field, and pressing anywhere begins the run. Ranks are drawn as altitude, any name replays as a ghost, and percentile leads over position.
After · the return
Reading these. Each .dc.html is a self-contained canvas — open it directly in a browser; support.js in this folder is the shared runtime and is not a design page. Directions are numbered in the order they were argued, and later ones cite earlier ones by number. Where two disagree, the higher-numbered revision wins and says so on its own canvas.
