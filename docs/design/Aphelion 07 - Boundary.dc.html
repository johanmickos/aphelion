APHELION  ·  DIRECTION 07  ·  THE BOUNDARY AS RISK GRADIENT
THE EDGE IS A PLACE,
NOT A WALL.
The most memorable discovery in a playtest was a scoring anomaly found outside the boundary by accident. That is the design brief in one sentence: the edge should be somewhere you aim at, not away from. So the boundary becomes three bands of increasing heat and reward, and its light answers a question no static vignette can: not "where is the edge" but "how fast are you approaching it."
THE THREE LAWS OF THE EDGE
Intensity = closing speed, not proximity. Coast along the outer band and the edge glows softly — you can live there. Dive at it and it flares. A wall reacts to where you are; a risk reacts to what you're doing.
Reward is shown, never spoken. Scoring motes drift in the outer bands, denser and brighter deeper in. No multiplier labels in the world, no arrows, no "RISK ZONE" text. The glimmer is the signpost.
The line is the only absolute. Bands are negotiable; the line is not. Past it, physics stops negotiating too — that stake is the game's one deliberate cruelty.
CROSS-SECTION — THE BANDS, PRICED
Why hugging is calm: a player skimming the fire band parallel to the line has near-zero closing speed, so the edge idles at a low ION glow — high reward, held nerve, quiet screen. The game respects the racing line instead of screaming at it.
Why diving is loud: closing speed multiplies the bloom, the deadline track (Direction 03) appears on the projected line with its recovery window, and fuel prices the save via lit-fraction. All three systems converge on the same 2 seconds — that convergence is the boundary experience.
LIVE — THE EDGE ANSWERS YOUR SPEED
The craft weaves toward and away from the line while climbing. Watch the edge: bright only while closing fast; calm when skimming. On a committed dive the deadline track appears — ION window on the projected line, dot = last press. Tweak aggression.
THE BURN — CROSSING, SURVIVED OR NOT
1 · THE CHOICE
Committed dive: deadline track live, window lit to the fuel's affordable fraction, edge flaring with closing speed. Everything true is on screen; nothing says "turn."
2 · THE SAVE — FLAMING BURN
Press inside the window: the craft carves back with an ION-tinged trail — the one time the player's white light wears pink, because the edge is writing on them. Fuel visibly drains from the halo during the carve. Survived skims become the game's best clips.
3 · THE LOSS
Missed press: SOS strobes, the craft's white core dissolves into ION embers at the line — the only death animation in the game, ~900ms, no slow-mo, no shake. Then the debrief: LEFT THE FIELD AT THE RIGHT WALL · ON FIRE.
WHY THIS STAYS AN INSTRUMENT
The boundary never issues a command. It states four facts — where the bands are (gradient in world space), what they pay (mote density), how fast you're closing (bloom), when saving stops being possible (deadline dot) — and lets the player price the trade.
ION keeps its monopoly: this is the only place the world glows pink, so peripheral vision alone knows the difference between "near planets" and "near the end of the world."
And the bands make the score-chase spatial: the optimal run lives in the fire band, the safe run in the field, and the leaderboard will show which players lived where — geography as skill expression, which is exactly what the daily needs (Direction 08).
THE BURN, ANIMATED — DEATH IS THE ANTI-RELEASE
tweak burnOutcome to lock a branch, burnRate to scrub slow
Same hitstop as the release — 70ms at the line. The release condenses your light into a word; the loss disperses it into the field. Mirrored grammar makes death feel like the game's own physics, not a punishment layer.
No explosion. The craft unravels along its velocity over ~900ms: stretch, core thinning, embers streaming strictly parallel behind. The streak rule holds even in death — nothing ever radiates.
SOS is a signal, not a scream. It strobes until the line, then stops. The silence after the last strobe is the loudest frame in the sequence.
The save is priced, visibly. The carve drains the fuel halo in real time and paints the trail ION — the only pink the player's light ever wears. No award word for surviving: the clip is the reward, and the debrief logs the skim.
BANK never counts down. On death it snaps to DUSK and the debrief states the number once. A draining counter is mockery; a stated fact is an epitaph.
