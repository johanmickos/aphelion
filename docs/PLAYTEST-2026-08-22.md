# Playtest review — 2026-08-22 phone capture

A design read of one recorded session, not a spec. This file is **history**: it
records what an 88-second capture showed on 2026-08-22 and what was concluded
from it. It describes no intended behaviour, so it cannot drift out of sync with
the code the way `DESIGN.md` and `FEEL.md` did — but for the same reason, do not
grow it into a standing design document. When one of the calls below is made, the
rationale goes next to the code that implements it, and this file stays as the
observation that prompted it.

## The artifact

`demo.mp4` — 88.8s, 1180×2556 portrait, 60fps HEVC, Safari on iOS against
`johanmickos.github.io`. Single unbroken run: 0 → 66,326, chain peaking at ×7.00,
ending in `LOST — OFF COURSE` at ~87s.

## Method

Everything below is measured off the capture, not impressions.

- Frames pulled at 1–8fps with `ffmpeg`, read as labelled contact sheets.
- Score sampled at 2fps: crop `520×66+290+407`, greyscale, contrast 2.2, negate,
  4× lanczos upscale, `tesseract --psm 7` with a digit whitelist. 132/164 frames
  in the play window produced a clean reading; cadence figures are from that
  series, cross-checked against a 1fps visual read of every second.
- Chain multiplier sampled the same way at 1fps.
- Audio checked with `showspectrumpic` and `astats`.

Reproduce with the commands in the sections below; the raw frames were scratch
and were not kept.

## What works

**The core loop.** Approach → hold → the orbit arc fills green-to-orange →
release at peak. The arc gauge drawn *on the orbit path* is the strongest piece
of UI in the game: diegetic, positioned exactly where the eye already is, and it
teaches the timing window without a word of text. It is the bar everything else
should be measured against.

**The teach.** Two lines on the title screen — "press and hold near a planet to
be caught by it / hold longer to orbit" — and they are sufficient. That is hard
to get right.

**The cadence, when it flows.** 49 payouts over 80 seconds, **median gap 1.5s**.
When the game is working it pays the player every second and a half.

**The anomaly's look.** The purple bloom, the world tint, the dashed red boundary
line read against it — 36s–47s is the only stretch of the capture that looks like
a finished game rather than a prototype.

**The off-screen planet markers** (edge triangles with `P19 843`) are the right
idea, and the boxed `⚠ LOST — OFF COURSE` label with a leader line to the ship is
a genuinely good piece of UI. Both are undermined by where and when they are
used — see below.

## What doesn't

### 1 — The HUD collides with the world, every frame

Highest damage, lowest cost to fix. Score and chain multiplier are drawn in the
same band as the off-screen planet distance markers, producing this at 52.5s:

```
                     28,240
              P21 84P20 57 51        <- planet labels through the score
                     x5.00
```

The OCR pass is unintentional evidence. Reading the multiplier at 1fps returned
`2216265`, `57492.008..`, `27442x5.02`, `933853x5.003x`. Those stray digits *are*
planet labels bleeding into the multiplier's box. If a program cannot segment it,
a thumb-height glance cannot either.

### 2 — Two different numbers are both rendered `x5.00`, adjacent

At 51s the chain drops 6.75 → 5.00 while the award line simultaneously reads
`+1,696  x6.75`. One award both *paid at* 6.75 and *reset the chain to* 5.00, and
the screen shows both with identical formatting one line apart:

```
26,658
  x5.00          <- the chain, after the reset
+1,696   x6.75   <- the bonus this award paid at
P17→P19  PEAK 0 · AIM 91
```

Determining which was which required stepping through frames.

### 3 — The chain multiplier did not move for a third of the run

Trajectory, per second:

| t | chain |
|---|---|
| 6s | ×1.25 |
| 12–34s | ×1.75 → ×4.00, climbing in ×0.25 steps |
| 36–41s | ×4.25 (held 6s — the anomaly) |
| 42s | ×6.50 — a +2.25 jump, unexplained on screen |
| 46–51s | ×6.75 |
| **52s** | **×5.00 — a drop, with no flash, shake, or sound** |
| 52–79s | **×5.00, unchanged for 27 seconds** |
| 80s | ×7.00 |
| 87s | dead |

Twenty-seven seconds — roughly a third of the session — during which the most
important progression number on screen said nothing. And the one moment it moved
*against* the player got less feedback than any single +1,500 award.

### 4 — 38% of the run is drought, and the worst of it is the set piece

Eight gaps of ≥3s with no payout, totalling 38% of playable time. The four
longest:

| window | silence |
|---|---|
| **36.5 → 42.5s** | **6.0s** — the anomaly |
| 73.0 → 77.5s | 4.5s |
| 47.0 → 51.0s | 4.0s |
| 56.0 → 60.0s | 4.0s |

The longest drought in the run is the anomaly encounter — the most spectacular
thing in the game is also its least rewarding stretch per second. Spectacle and
reward point in opposite directions at the exact moment the player is most
engaged.

### 5 — The praise vocabulary's axis structure is right; its synonym depth defeats it

`src/score/praise.ts` holds **31 unique words** across five categories:

| category | tier 1 | tier 2 | unique |
|---|---|---|---|
| `close` | 4 | 3 | 7 |
| `nerve` | 4 | (same list) | 4 |
| `aim` | 4 | 4 | 8 |
| `peak` | 3 | 3 | 6 |
| `super` | 3 (grab) | 3 (release) | 6 |

The design intent — every word names its own axis, no caption needed — is sound,
and the header comment shows it was reasoned about. What defeats it in play is
**3–4 synonyms per tier**. This capture showed 14 distinct words — SNAPPED,
TRACKING, CATAPULT, FLAWLESS, REDLINE, SURGICAL, DEADEYE, THREADED, IMMACULATE,
TEXTBOOK, WHIPPED, CLUTCH, TIMED, SLINGSHOT — and **almost every one appeared
exactly once**, a new word roughly every 6 seconds.

A player cannot learn that TRACKING and DEADEYE are the same axis when they see
each once per run. With that much synonym depth, a given axis-tier presents a
*different* word nearly every time it fires, so no word ever becomes a signal.
Colour ends up doing all the work and the vocabulary does none — the player
learns "gold text = good" and stops there.

### 6 — The ending is the worst moment in the game

| t | state |
|---|---|
| 85.5s | +2,292 · score 66,326 · ×7.00 · fuel 100% |
| ~87.0s | `LOST — OFF COURSE` · score 0 |
| ~87.5s | already respawned on a fresh map |

About one second, no ceremony, no run summary, no beat. The best run of the
session evaporates, and the `BEST 66,326` that replaces it renders in the same
tiny grey as the debug readout, adjacent to a `0`, in a way the eye cannot
separate.

The warning is a right-edge red gradient going from faint to fatal in ~1.5s — no
distance, no countdown, no turn-back vector. At the moment of death the ship had
**full fuel and two planets on screen**, so from the player's seat the failure
reads as arbitrary.

The irony: the boxed `⚠ LOST — OFF COURSE` label with its leader line is exactly
the right treatment. It is currently the obituary. It should be the warning.

### 7 — The game is silent

`showspectrumpic` over the full track shows broadband noise with no tonal
structure and no impulses at score events — room tone from the capture, nothing
else. For a game whose entire skill expression is *timing a release*, this is the
largest missing feature. A pitch ramp through the boost arc, peaking at the
release window, would teach the core mechanic better than the arc gauge does, and
would free the player to watch the ship instead of the gauge.

### 8 — Screen real estate is inverted

- **Fuel** gets a large 10-segment bar in the bottom-left. It never dropped below
  ~58 and sat at 100 for most of the run. It was never a decision.
- **Score and chain** are jammed into the top strip under the browser URL bar —
  the hardest place to glance at on a phone — sharing space with planet labels.
- The two quantities the player trades against each other sit in **opposite
  corners** of a six-inch screen.
- `TUNE / RESET / DEBUG` occupy the bottom bar, the most thumb-reachable strip on
  the device.

## Recommendations

### Unambiguous fixes — no design call needed

1. **Reserve a HUD band with an occlusion mask** so no world label draws into it.
2. **Stop calling two things `x`.** The award line's figure is a *bonus*; the
   persistent one is the *chain*. Different glyph, colour, and position.
3. **Punctuate the chain break.** Whatever costs 1.75× deserves more feedback
   than a single +1,500 award gets.
4. **Fire `LOST — OFF COURSE` as a warning**, ~3s from the boundary, same boxed
   treatment, with a turn-back vector and a countdown.

### Design calls — measure and confirm before building

5. **A death beat.** Freeze, pull the camera back over the run's path, show final
   against best. How long it holds, and whether the respawn stays automatic, is a
   feel decision.
6. **Cut synonym depth in `praise.ts`**, keeping all five axes and both tiers —
   roughly 1–2 words per tier instead of 3–4, so each axis recurs often enough to
   become learnable. Which words survive is the author's call. Note that
   thresholds are measured, never chosen: this is a re-measure at coarser
   granularity, not a re-pick.
7. **Audio.** Pitch ramp on the boost arc, a click at the grab, a swell at the
   release. Largest gameplay return per hour of anything in this list.
8. **Rebalance the HUD to the thumb.** Chain and score belong low and central;
   fuel earns its size only if it is ever a real constraint.

### Not yet investigated

- Whether the ×4.25 → ×6.50 jump at 42s is an anomaly bonus, and whether anything
  on screen is meant to announce it.
- Whether the ×5.00 plateau is a cap or a decay/gain equilibrium.
- The coast phase between planets is roughly half the loop and currently accepts
  no input. Whether that is dead time or necessary breathing room is a feel
  question this capture cannot settle on its own.
